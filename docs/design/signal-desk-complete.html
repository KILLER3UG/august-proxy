Here are the notes. Everything below is derived from the same usage.csv window as the chart, so the numbers in the formulas and the table always agree.

How a day gets flagged

A day is flagged when its distance from the mean, measured in standard deviations, crosses the gate:

z = x − μ σ 1

Plugging Tuesday in: z = (4.21M − 1.08M) / 0.34M ≈ 9.2 — far beyond the |z| > 2.5 gate, so the day is flagged. Qwen and Claude stayed under |z| < 1 all month.

Where σ comes from

σ = √ 1N NΣi=1 (xi − μ)2 2

Week-over-week growth uses the same pieces — the Δ column in the table below is formula (3) applied to the daily totals:

gt = xt − xt−1 xt−1 · 100% 3

Remember

The 2.5σ gate crosses on roughly 1% of ordinary days — in a 30-day window, expect zero or one flag. A flag is a prompt to read the run ledger, not an alarm.

Definitions

μ
The mean — average daily tokens across the window. Here 1.08M.
σ
The standard deviation — the typical distance from μ. Here 0.34M; the square root of the variance σ².
z
A z-score — distance from the mean measured in σ. z = 0 is an ordinary day; |z| > 2.5 is a spike.

¹ μ and σ are computed on the 29 ordinary days — a day is never part of its own baseline. Worked example in Canvas → Notes.
